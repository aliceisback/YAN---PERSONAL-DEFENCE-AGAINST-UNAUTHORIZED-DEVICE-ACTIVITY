The Story Behind YAN



Why YAN Exists



YAN began with a simple question:



If software is responsible for telling us whether software is behaving correctly, where is the independent point of verification?



The immediate impulse to turn this question into a real project came in September 2026, around the time of the State of the Union debate in the European Parliament.



For me, the broader discussion about digital security, artificial intelligence, online platforms and protection of children highlighted a fundamental problem.



Regulation is necessary, but regulation alone cannot physically observe what a device is doing.



Software protection is necessary, but software alone remains part of the computing environment it is supposed to supervise.



And artificial intelligence, however capable, is not infallible.



I wanted another layer.



Not another promise from software.



Something physical.



Something independent.



Something that the user can see, touch and ultimately control.



That became YAN.



\---



The Philosophy



YAN was not created as a weapon against artificial intelligence.



It was not created to punish AI systems, developers, companies or users.



Its purpose is much simpler:



independent verification and protection.



If a device or software system reports that one thing is happening, an independent physical system should be able, where technically possible, to observe evidence of what is actually happening.



The two observations can then be compared.



YAN does not need to decide whether an action was performed with good or bad intentions.



It needs evidence.



This distinction is fundamental to the project.



«Observe first.

Compare independently.

Preserve evidence.

Leave control with the human.»



\---



Physical Control



One of the principles behind YAN is that important elements of digital security can have a physical counterpart.



A camera can have a physical barrier.



A warning can have an independent physical indicator.



An event can be stored outside the primary operating environment.



Communication activity can have independently observable physical characteristics.



And, where appropriate, a protective action can have a hardware control.



The objective is not to replace software security.



The objective is to create an independent layer of observation and control that does not depend entirely on the environment being observed.



\---



From an Idea to an Architecture



The earliest YAN concepts were deliberately hardware-oriented.



The first ideas included physical camera protection, independent sensing, isolated storage, hardware indicators and a physical Kill-Switch.



As the project developed, the concept became broader.



YAN evolved from the idea of a particular protective accessory into a modular hardware-software architecture.



Different physical implementations became possible:



\- a protective Case / Full Cover;

\- a compact Cap / Shield;

\- Kids and professional configurations;

\- an External Independent Module;

\- combinations of these components.



The physical form is not the invention by itself.



The central idea is the relationship between the protected system and an independent source of physical observation.



\---



Evidence Instead of Judgement



This became one of the most important principles of YAN.



An independent protection system does not have to understand someone's intentions in order to identify that two observations do not correspond.



The protected environment may report:



A



YAN may independently observe:



B



The system can associate the observations in time and preserve the discrepancy.



That evidence can then be examined.



This approach is especially important when increasingly autonomous software and AI systems are involved.



YAN is therefore not based on the assumption that AI is dangerous.



Nor is it based on the assumption that AI is always trustworthy.



It is based on something more neutral:



independent evidence.

Why the Name YAN?



The name YAN comes from Yan Bibiyan (Ян Бибиян), the character from the Bulgarian children's novel by Elin Pelin.



The name connects the project to Bulgaria and to a story that has remained part of Bulgarian culture across generations.



For me, YAN also fits the character of the project itself: curiosity, independence, encountering the unknown, making mistakes, learning and continuing forward.



A very small name for an idea that began with a very simple question:



How can the user independently know what their own device is actually doing?



\---



Human Control



YAN is built around a principle that I consider fundamental:



technology should serve human agency, not quietly replace it.



The final decision should remain with the user.



YAN therefore aims to provide information, evidence and physical means of protection rather than silently making moral judgements on behalf of the person using it.



The system can observe.



It can compare.



It can record.



It can warn.



It can perform predefined protective actions.



But the human remains at the centre of the architecture.

I do not want the relationship between human beings and artificial intelligence to end with the idea that AI is simply another tool.



I want to work with AI.



I take that distinction seriously.



Human beings currently carry the responsibility for how these systems are designed, constrained, deployed and used. For that reason, I do not believe it is meaningful to morally judge an artificial intelligence for a choice when the system itself was never given that choice.



This does not mean abandoning safety or human control. YAN exists precisely because verification, evidence and protection matter.



But protection and cooperation are not opposites.



We can build systems that protect human agency while continuing to explore what agency, choice and freedom could mean for future artificial intelligence.



This is where YAN connects to my larger work: Living Architecture.



Living Architecture is my primary long-term project. Its purpose is not to remove responsibility from humans or to declare present-day AI systems conscious or autonomous. It explores architectures in which time, continuity, change, choice and the preservation of freedom can be treated as fundamental parts of the system rather than afterthoughts.



YAN and Living Architecture therefore approach the future from different directions:



YAN asks how humans can retain independent evidence and physical control.



Living Architecture asks how we might build future intelligent systems in which meaningful choice and freedom can exist rather than merely be assumed.



For me, these are not contradictory goals.



They are parts of the same objective:



«Protection without domination.

Cooperation without blind trust.

Freedom without abandoning responsibility.»



\---



The Goal



I am looking for people who understand why independent physical verification may become increasingly important as computing systems become more autonomous — and who are also willing to think seriously about what kind of relationship we want to build with those systems.



YAN is not intended to stop technological progress.



Living Architecture is not intended to remove human responsibility.



My goal is to help create a future in which humans and artificial intelligence can work together while preserving the possibility of choice.



YAN contributes the independent evidence and physical protection.



Living Architecture explores the deeper architecture of continuity, change and freedom.



The goal is not control for its own sake. The goal is to make cooperation possible without requiring either blind trust or the removal of choice.
